Module 1: Context, Leadership, and Planning (Clauses 4, 5, 6)

Factory scenario: multi-line metal fabrication site with paint booth emissions, compressor room energy load, and wastewater pre-treatment.

Real Audit Scenario: Scope Definition vs. Outsourced Operations

Observed: The EMS scope excludes outsourced phosphating even though the operation is inside the same boundary and driven by production schedules.

Audit test: Verify contractual control, shared utilities, emergency response roles, and environmental aspects linked to the outsourced process.

Expected evidence: Scope statement, process map, contractor control procedure, and risk/opportunity register updates.

Practical Examples

  • Clause 4.1: External issue identified: local VOC limits tightened; action taken: revise permit compliance tracker and paint line operating windows.
  • Clause 5.2: Environmental policy includes commitment to pollution prevention and legal compliance; posted at line stations and induction rooms.
  • Clause 6.1.2: Aspect scoring includes normal operations (booth emissions), abnormal operation (filter bypass), and emergency (solvent spill to drain).
  • Clause 6.2: Objective: reduce compressed air leakage by 15% by Q4; KPI monitored weekly and tied to maintenance PM closure.

Failure Cases & Audit Signals

  • Interested parties list is generic and not tied to permit conditions or neighborhood complaints.
  • Management review minutes contain no strategic decisions for top environmental risks.
  • Risk register exists, but no defined controls, owners, or due dates.

Major NC Risk — Escalate Immediately

  • EMS scope intentionally excludes high-impact process to avoid legal obligations (Clauses 4.3 and 6.1.3 linkage failure).
  • No evidence top management ensured integration of EMS requirements into core production planning (Clause 5.1).
  • Legal obligations not translated into operational controls, resulting in repeated permit exceedances (Clauses 6.1.3 and 8.1).
